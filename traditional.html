Traditional Instruments

Instruments

Unlike Western orchestras grouped strictly by string, brass, and woodwind, traditional Chinese instruments are classified under the ancient Bayin (Eight Tones) system based on their raw material: Silk, Bamboo, Wood, Stone, Metal, Clay, Gourd, and Hide.

Silk (Si)

The Erhu (二胡)

Two-Stringed Bowed Fiddle

Featuring a python-skin soundbox and a bow threaded completely between its two strings, the Erhu acts as the "violin" of China. Lacking a rigid wooden fingerboard, strings are pressed directly in mid-air, allowing players to create fluid, deep glissandos that beautifully mirror crying, laughing, or expressive vocal slides.

HEAR SOLO EXAMPLES
Silk (Si)

The Guzheng (古筝)

21-String Plucked Zither

The Guzheng features 21 movable tuning bridges stretched across a large wooden body. Musicians pluck melodic patterns with their right hand using tortoiseshell picks, while the left hand presses down behind the bridges to alter string tension—producing distinct, immediate ornamentation, wide note bends, and water-like glissandos.

EXPLORE NOTATION
Bamboo (Zhu)

The Dizi (笛子)

Transverse Bamboo Flute

The Dizi is a classic side-blown bamboo flute containing an extra blowhole lined with a micro-thin reed membrane called a dimo. When air passes across this membrane, it adds a characteristically bright, clearing, buzzing ring to the timbre that allows the melody to cut through vast outdoor country landscapes.

FINGERING CHART
Silk (Si)

The Pipa (琵琶)

Four-Stringed Plucked Lute

Held upright on the lap, this pear-shaped lute features a fully fretted neck. The Pipa is famous for its dynamic percussive intensity; players use a signature five-finger rolling pluck technique (Pila) to generate massive waves of sound, making it perfect for dramatic, historical narrative battle pieces.

TECHNIQUE GUIDE
Gourd (Pao)

The Sheng (笙)

Traditional Reed Mouth Organ

One of the world's oldest polyphonic instruments, the Sheng consists of multiple vertical bamboo pipes mounted inside a wind chest. By blowing into the mouthpiece and closing fingerholes, players can sound clean, continuous vertical chords. It acts as the rich harmonic glue holding folk ensembles together.

Wood / Metal

The Suona (唢呐)

Double-Reed Conical Horn

Equipped with a metallic brass bell and a double-reed mouthpiece, the Suona generates a raw, piercingly loud volume. Widely favored in rural festival marches, weddings, and funeral arrangements, it excels at imitating high-pitched bird calls and driving celebratory energy across open spaces.
