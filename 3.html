Xiāng Sī

By Wang Wei [Tang Dynasty 8th Century]

Wang Wei is a renowned poet and painter of the Tang Dynasty whose works are still known throughout China. Wang Wei was from the Han people.

Children learn Wang Wei's poems at home and then again in school. This arrangement of Xiāng Sī is one of many musical arrangements of his poem. It is a song of remembrance and may be sung at any time of year.

Technical Musical Analysis

Melody & Pitch: Built entirely on a five-tone pentatonic scale framework, omitting standard Western semitones. The horizontal line relies on fluid pitch bends and portamento to track linguistic tones.
Texture & Space: Bypasses chord progressions in favor of a sparse, transparent heterophonic texture. Voices and instruments mirror the same melody with subtle variations, prioritizing structural silence.
Timbre & Tone: Emphasizes bright, acoustic instrument color profiles rooted in natural elements like wood, bamboo, and silk, backed by a forward nasal vocal production with minimal vibrato.

Pinyin Version

Hóng dòu shēng nán guó,

chūn lái fā jǐ zhī.

Yuàn jūn duō cǎi xié yà,

cǐ wù zuì xiāng sī.

English Translation

The red beans grow in the southland

Flourish on the branches when spring comes

Would you gather an armful, I pray

For the fond love and remembrance they express.

Chinese Text

《相思》    唐朝·王维

红豆生南国，

春来发几枝。

劝君多采撷，

此物最相思。
